## 5.6 Luna

This page is a starting point. It covers the main patterns we saw when we tested 5.6 Luna against GPT-4.1 on several real assistants. Your assistant may behave differently in ways our tests didn't show, so test your own key scenarios before you switch.

**Recommended settings:** set these fields inside your assistant's existing `model` object, and keep its other fields, such as `messages` and `tools`, as they are.

```json
{
  "model": {
    "provider": "openai",
    "model": "gpt-5.6-luna",
    "reasoningEffort": "low",
    "serviceTier": "fast"
  }
}
```

We ran GPT-4.1 and 5.6 Luna on the same simulated calls across several real assistants. These are tendencies, not things that happen on every call, ordered by how much they're likely to affect your calls.

### Point each branch to your closing steps

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna follows the instruction for the step it's on, so a step that ends with "confirm and close the call" can close the call there and skip global steps written elsewhere, such as a closing question or a link to send. GPT-4.1 kept those general rules in mind for the whole call. If this matters for your calls, Sapien or 6.1 Terra may suit them better.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can write the closing steps and exceptions once, and replace each branch's "close the call", declined offers included, with a pointer that names the next step, like "Then send the link and follow # Closing."

### Write exactly what you mean

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna reads the prompt's words more literally than GPT-4.1, so an optional "you can offer" may be skipped and a missing rule answered by the letter, where GPT-4.1 worked out what was meant.
- **For your prompt:** Write each step the way you want it read, for example "offer the link" rather than "you can offer the link" when it's required, and check that every status the prompt checks is one your tools return.

### Aim for good prompt coverage

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna doesn't invent answers the way GPT-4.1 sometimes did. When the prompt doesn't cover a situation, it can hold back and take the broadest option your setup gives it, which on some assistants meant more transfers or callbacks than the call needed. If this matters for your calls, 5.6 Terra may suit them better.
- **For your prompt:** Aim for good coverage of the workflows a call can go down, writing what to say or do, for example "Asking to speed up a refund doesn't qualify: give the expected date." Test that nearby requests still reach a person.

### Write complete tool descriptions

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna gives tool and parameter descriptions more weight than GPT-4.1 did, and a description that disagrees with the prompt can win. On one assistant it called a finish tool whose description said to use it when the conversation was complete, even though the prompt said never to call it.
- **For your prompt:** Give every tool and parameter a complete description that matches the prompt, for example "Payment type. Always ARBITRARY: the amount is whatever the caller chose." This worked in our tests.

### Plan for warmth

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna sounds more neutral than GPT-4.1, with turns about a third shorter and less enthusiasm, and callers on one assistant found it colder until the prompt wrote in reasons, alternatives and next steps. On one assistant, it followed a rule that limits acknowledgments, which GPT-4.1 often ignored.
- **For your prompt:** Loosen or narrow rules that ban acknowledgments, and when the assistant has to say no, have it explain why, offer one alternative and say what happens next, rather than relying on a general line like "be warm".

### Write the next step after each answer

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna answers what the caller asked and then waits for the caller to lead, one of the tendencies we saw most often, and rarely asks "anything else?" or offers a link unless the current step tells it to.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can write the next step after each kind of answer and each tool result, for example "After a successful payment, ask whether there's anything else you can help with."

### Plan for tool failures

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna follows written steps strictly and in order, and keeps counted steps, such as "transfer on the third request", more closely than GPT-4.1, though not always exactly. When a tool fails and the prompt has no recovery step, it can stop or send the caller elsewhere instead of improvising.
- **For your prompt:** Write a fallback line for each tool failure that matters, ending with the next step as a question, for example "No payment was made. Would you like me to text you a secure link?" This worked in our tests.

### Mark lines that must be said exactly

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna repeats some sample lines word for word, so calls can start to sound the same, and can trim or reword a longer required script to fit other rules.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can mark lines that must be said exactly with "Say exactly:", exempt them from rules that would cut or rewrite them, and for lines that should vary, describe what they should contain.

### Attach only the tools you need

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Luna runs the lookups and verification steps the prompt requires, which GPT-4.1 often skipped. It may also call attached tools the prompt never mentions.
- **For your prompt:** Attach only the tools the assistant actually needs.

### Pair each line with its tool call

- **What we saw:** Around transfers, texts and ending the call, 5.6 Luna can say it's connecting the caller without calling the tool, and sometimes calls a tool without saying anything first, which the caller hears as silence. It stops talking after a transfer or end-call tool and rarely claims an action no tool confirmed. If this matters for your calls, Terra may suit them better.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can tie the line and the tool call together in one instruction, for example "Say 'Let me transfer you to billing.' and call `transfer_call` in the same response.", and keep transfer wording in refusal lines.

## 6 Luna

This page is a starting point. It covers the main patterns we saw when we tested 6 Luna against GPT-4.1 on several real assistants. Your assistant may behave differently in ways our tests didn't show, so test your own key scenarios before you switch.

**Recommended settings:** set these fields inside your assistant's existing `model` object, and keep its other fields, such as `messages` and `tools`, as they are.

```json
{
  "model": {
    "provider": "openai",
    "model": "gpt-6-luna",
    "reasoningEffort": "low",
    "serviceTier": "fast"
  }
}
```

We ran GPT-4.1 and 6 Luna on the same simulated calls across several real assistants. These are tendencies, not things that happen on every call, ordered by how much they're likely to affect your calls.

### Pair each line with its tool call

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna can call a tool, such as a lookup or a transfer, without saying anything first more often than GPT-4.1, which the caller hears as silence. On one assistant, at the end of a call it also said goodbye without calling the end-call tool, or ended the call without a word. If this matters for your calls, Terra may suit them better.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can put the line in a lookup's start message, for example "One second here, while I pull up your account.", and test that the transfer line and any safety sentence come before the transfer connects.

### Point each branch to your closing steps

- **What we saw:** When a step in the prompt has its own last line, 6 Luna can follow it and drop closing steps written elsewhere, which GPT-4.1 kept in mind for the whole call. On some assistants it ended the call while the caller still had an open request, or handed the call back instead of closing it. If this matters for your calls, Sapien or 6.1 Terra may suit them better.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can write the closing steps once, before the end tool, and replace each branch's "close the call" with a pointer that names the next step, such as "Then send the link and follow # Closing."

### Write complete tool descriptions

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna gives tool descriptions, parameter options and the text in tool results more weight than GPT-4.1 did. It can call a tool whose description invites it, such as a finish or end tool, even when the prompt says not to.
- **For your prompt:** Write a complete description for every tool and parameter that agrees with the prompt, for example "Payment type. Always ARBITRARY", and keep each script in one place, not repeated in a tool result. This worked in our tests.

### Attach only the tools you need

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna runs the lookups and verification steps the prompt requires, which GPT-4.1 often skipped. It can also call attached tools the prompt never mentions.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can attach only the tools the assistant actually needs, for example detach a `finish` tool your prompt says never to call, then test calls that take payments on your own assistant.

### Aim for good prompt coverage

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna doesn't invent answers the way GPT-4.1 sometimes did. When the prompt doesn't cover a situation, it can take the broadest, safest option the setup gives it and transfer or escalate more often, sometimes for routine requests. On a scheduling assistant it sent some routine calls, such as a caller booking a visit for a few weeks of knee pain, to its emergency route.
- **For your prompt:** Aim for good coverage of the workflows a call can go down, writing what to say or do and when not to escalate, for example "Address changes aren't a reason to transfer: repeat the email." This worked in our tests.

### Match names and labels exactly

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna follows the exact wording of conditions and labels more closely than GPT-4.1. If the prompt says to act on accounts marked "OPEN", an account marked "LATE" falls outside that rule.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can check that every value and label the prompt checks matches what your tools actually return, for example name every status that can take a payment ("OPEN or LATE"), not just one.

### Mark lines that must be said exactly

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna says short sample lines word for word more often than GPT-4.1, but it can trim or reword a longer required script to fit other rules in the prompt.
- **For your prompt:** Give two or three different labeled examples, and mark lines that must be said exactly with "Say exactly:", exempting them from rules that would rewrite them. This worked in our tests.

### Write in the warmth you want

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna sounds plainer than GPT-4.1, with shorter turns and almost no exclamation marks or filler. On some assistants it added less sympathy of its own and relied on lines the prompt writes for it.
- **For your prompt:** Write acknowledgments into the prompt for the moments that matter, such as bad news or a caller in distress, and when the assistant can't do what was asked, have it explain why, offer one alternative and say what happens next.

### Plan for tool failures

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna keeps the guardrails and checks the prompt requires, where GPT-4.1 sometimes bent them for simple requests. It doesn't claim an action succeeded when a tool failed.
- **For your prompt:** Write a fallback line for each tool failure that matters, ending with the next step as a question, for example "No payment was made. Would you like me to text you a secure link?" This worked in our tests.

### Write the next step after each answer

- **What we saw:** 6 Luna answers what the caller asked and then waits, and rarely asks "anything else?" unless the current step tells it to, where GPT-4.1 often offered the next step on its own.
- **For your prompt:** Write the next step after each kind of answer and each tool result, for example "After a successful payment, ask whether there's anything else you can help with." This worked in our tests.
