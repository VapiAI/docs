## 6.1 Terra

This page is a starting point. It covers the main patterns we saw when we tested 6.1 Terra against GPT-4.1 on several real assistants. Your assistant may behave differently in ways our tests didn't show, so test your own key scenarios before you switch.

**Recommended settings:** set these fields inside your assistant's existing `model` object, and keep its other fields, such as `messages` and `tools`, as they are.

```json
{
  "model": {
    "provider": "openai",
    "model": "gpt-6.1-terra",
    "reasoningEffort": "low",
    "serviceTier": "fast"
  }
}
```

We ran GPT-4.1 and 6.1 Terra on the same simulated calls across several real assistants. These are tendencies, not things that happen on every call, ordered by how much they're likely to affect your calls.

### Match names and triggers exactly

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra can match field names, labels and trigger words exactly as the prompt writes them, more than GPT-4.1, so a prompt that checked a `verified` field while the tool returned `"state": "VERIFIED"` led it to treat verified callers as unverified. On some assistants a rule triggered by the word "Spanish" didn't fire for callers who simply spoke Spanish, and a list of urgent keywords applied to every mention of those words.
- **For your prompt:** Name each tool-result field exactly as the tool returns it, where the decision is made, and describe triggers by meaning, such as "if the caller's first message is in Spanish or asks for Spanish". This worked in our tests.

### Aim for good prompt coverage

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra doesn't invent answers the way GPT-4.1 sometimes did. When the prompt doesn't cover a situation, it can say it isn't sure, sometimes with no next step, or offer a person sooner than GPT-4.1 did.
- **For your prompt:** Aim for good coverage of the workflows a call can go down, writing what to say or do, for example "Asking to speed up a refund doesn't qualify: give the expected date." Test that nearby requests still reach a person.

### Say where each closing step applies

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra can apply a global closing step in every branch, even where it doesn't fit, such as offering a survey after a transfer, where GPT-4.1 usually skipped it.
- **For your prompt:** Give each closing step one place, state its scope there, and tie the order to the end-call tool, for example "Call `end_call` only after the caller answers the survey question." This worked in our tests.

### Write complete tool descriptions

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra gives tool descriptions, parameter descriptions and the text in tool results more weight than GPT-4.1 did. When a tool returns text meant for the caller, it can read it out right away and then say the prompt's version too.
- **For your prompt:** Write tool descriptions that agree with the prompt and keep each script in one place, at a step every call passes, for example "Right after the name check, before any transfer, say the notice." This worked in our tests.

### Attach only the tools you need

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra looks things up before answering, sometimes even when the question doesn't need it, where GPT-4.1 often answered from memory. It can also call attached tools the prompt never mentions.
- **For your prompt:** Attach only the tools the assistant needs, and when you list questions that need no lookup, name the ones that still do, for example "A PIN reset needs no lookup; card arrival still does." This worked in our tests.

### Plan for tool failures

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra follows written steps strictly and in order, more than GPT-4.1, and keeps counted steps, such as "transfer on the third request", exactly. It doesn't claim an action succeeded when a tool failed.
- **For your prompt:** In the step that calls a tool, give the failures that matter a fallback line and next step, for example "If it fails, say you couldn't set it up just now, then send the link." This worked in our tests.

### Mark lines that must be said exactly

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra says short sample lines word for word more often than GPT-4.1, but it can reword a required script to fit a general rule, such as a rule to say numbers as words.
- **For your prompt:** Mark lines that must be said exactly with "Say exactly:" and exempt them from rules that would rewrite them, such as a numbers-as-words rule. This worked in our tests. For a line that should vary, describe it, don't quote it.

### Plan for warmth

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra sounds plainer than GPT-4.1, with shorter turns and far fewer exclamation marks and filler. It adds a short acknowledgment of bad news more often than GPT-4.1 did, and follows rules that limit acknowledgments exactly.
- **For your prompt:** Loosen or narrow rules that ban acknowledgments, and when the assistant can't do what was asked, have it explain why, offer one alternative and say what happens next.

### Pair each line with its tool call

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra usually says its line before a transfer, but on some assistants it called a tool before saying anything, which the caller hears as silence. We didn't see it claim an action that no tool confirmed.
- **For your prompt:** Tie the line and the tool call together in one instruction, for example: "Say 'Let me transfer you to billing.' and call `transfer_call` in the same response." This worked in our tests.

### Write the next step after each answer

- **What we saw:** 6.1 Terra answers what the caller asked and then waits unless a step in the prompt says what comes next, where GPT-4.1 often offered the next step on its own.
- **For your prompt:** Write the next step after each kind of answer, for example "After a successful payment, ask whether there's anything else you can help with." This worked in our tests.

## 5.6 Terra

This page is a starting point. It covers the main patterns we saw when we tested 5.6 Terra against GPT-4.1 on several real assistants. Your assistant may behave differently in ways our tests didn't show, so test your own key scenarios before you switch.

**Recommended settings:** set these fields inside your assistant's existing `model` object, and keep its other fields, such as `messages` and `tools`, as they are.

```json
{
  "model": {
    "provider": "openai",
    "model": "gpt-5.6-terra",
    "reasoningEffort": "low",
    "serviceTier": "fast"
  }
}
```

We ran GPT-4.1 and 5.6 Terra on the same simulated calls across several real assistants. These are tendencies, not things that happen on every call, ordered by how much they're likely to affect your calls.

### Point each branch to your closing steps

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra follows the instruction for the step it's on, so a step that ends with "confirm and close the call" can close the call there and skip global steps written elsewhere, such as a closing question or a link to send. GPT-4.1 kept those general rules in mind for the whole call.
- **For your prompt:** To help reduce this, you can write the closing steps once and replace each branch's "close the call", failures included, with a pointer that names the next step, such as "Then send the link and follow # Closing."

### Match names and triggers exactly

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra does what the prompt says without inferring what the author meant, more than GPT-4.1. When a rule switched languages only if the caller asked for Spanish, it could keep answering in English to a caller who simply spoke Spanish.
- **For your prompt:** Describe triggers by meaning, for example "if the caller's first message is in Spanish or asks for Spanish", and check that every name in the prompt matches your configuration. This worked in our tests for language switches.

### Aim for good prompt coverage

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra doesn't invent answers the way GPT-4.1 sometimes did. When the prompt doesn't cover a situation, it can hold back and take the broadest option your setup gives it, which on some assistants meant more transfers or callbacks than the call needed.
- **For your prompt:** Aim for good coverage of the workflows a call can go down, writing what to say or do beside its rule, for example "Asking to speed up a refund doesn't qualify: give the expected date." This worked in our tests.

### Write complete tool descriptions

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra gives tool and parameter descriptions more weight than GPT-4.1 did, and a description that disagrees with the prompt can win. On one assistant it called a finish tool whose description said to use it when the conversation was complete, even though the prompt said never to call it.
- **For your prompt:** Give every tool and parameter a complete description that matches the prompt, for example "The phone number from # Applicant on File, exactly as written there, including the leading +1." This worked in our tests.

### Plan for warmth

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra sounds more neutral than GPT-4.1, with turns about a third shorter and little enthusiasm or filler, so it can sound colder unless the prompt writes in the warmth you want. On one assistant, it followed a rule that limits acknowledgments, which GPT-4.1 often ignored.
- **For your prompt:** Loosen or narrow rules that ban acknowledgments, and when the assistant has to say no, have it explain why, offer one alternative and say what happens next, rather than relying on a general line like "be warm".

### Write the next step after each answer

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra answers what the caller asked and then waits, and rarely asks "anything else?" or offers a link unless the current step tells it to, where GPT-4.1 offered the next step on its own.
- **For your prompt:** Write the next step after each kind of answer and each tool result, for example "After a successful payment, ask whether there's anything else you can help with." This worked in our tests.

### Plan for tool failures

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra follows written steps strictly and in order, more than GPT-4.1, and keeps counted steps, such as "transfer on the third request", exactly. When a tool fails and the prompt has no recovery step, it can stop or send the caller elsewhere instead of improvising, and when the prompt had one, such as "retry once, then read this line", it followed it.
- **For your prompt:** Write a fallback line for each tool failure that matters, ending with the next step as a question, for example "No payment was made. Would you like me to text you a secure link?" This worked in our tests.

### Mark lines that must be said exactly

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra says sample lines from the prompt word for word more often than GPT-4.1, so calls can start to sound the same.
- **For your prompt:** Label examples clearly and give two or three different ones, and mark lines that must be said exactly with "Say exactly:", making sure no other rule contradicts them. This worked in our tests.

### Attach only the tools you need

- **What we saw:** 5.6 Terra runs the lookups and verification steps the prompt requires, which GPT-4.1 often skipped. It may also call attached tools the prompt never mentions.
- **For your prompt:** Attach only the tools the assistant actually needs, for example detach a `finish` tool your prompt says never to call. This worked in our tests.

### Pair each line with its tool call

- **What we saw:** Around transfers and texts, and most often for the first lookup in a call, 5.6 Terra can call a tool without saying anything first, which the caller hears as silence. It stops talking after a transfer or end-call tool and rarely claims an action no tool confirmed.
- **For your prompt:** Tie each line to its tool call in one instruction, for example "Say 'Let me transfer you to billing.' and call `transfer_call` in the same response.", and script the line before a call's first lookup. This worked in our tests.
