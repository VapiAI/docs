GPT-Live is an OpenAI voice model that listens and speaks at the same time. In a Vapi assistant, it handles the spoken conversation while a separate reasoner looks things up and takes actions through your tools. While the work runs, the assistant can keep listening, ask a useful question, or answer something else.

Here's an illustrative call to the example business used throughout these guides. Its assistant has tools to check and book appointment times, and its prompt says how long each service takes. The background notes show what the reasoner is doing alongside the conversation.

> **Caller:** Hi, I need a consultation at your downtown location this Friday. Late afternoon if you can.
>
> **Assistant:** Sure, I'll look at Friday downtown. While I check, what name should the booking be under?
>
> *Background: checking Friday's open times downtown.*
>
> **Caller:** Sam Lee. Oh, and how long does it take? I have to pick up my son at five.
>
> **Assistant:** A consultation is thirty minutes.
>
> *Background: the open times come back: 10:30, 2:30, and 4.*
>
> **Assistant:** For Friday afternoon I have 2:30 or 4. Four would finish around 4:30. Would that leave you enough time to get there?
>
> **Caller:** Yes, the school's ten minutes away. Four, then.
>
> **Assistant:** That's Friday at 4, downtown, under Sam Lee. Shall I book it?
>
> **Caller:** Yes, please.
>
> *Background: the booking service confirms Friday at 4, downtown, under Sam Lee.*
>
> **Assistant:** You're booked for Friday at 4 downtown.

A few things happen here that a builder designs for:

- **The wait is used.** The assistant starts the lookup as soon as it knows the service, location, and date, and asks for the name while the lookup runs. It answers the caller's question from what its prompt already tells it.
- **The result fits the conversation.** The caller mentioned the five o'clock pickup while the lookup was running. When the times come back, the assistant offers the afternoon ones and asks whether the later one leaves enough time, instead of assuming.
- **Choosing isn't booking.** "Four, then" is a choice. The assistant reads back the details and books only after the caller says yes. It says "You're booked" only once the booking service has confirmed it.

[Design conversations](/gpt-live/design) explains how to shape these moments in your own prompts.

## How it works

A GPT-Live assistant has two parts:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Speaker" icon="fa-light fa-microphone-lines">
    The voice the caller hears. It listens and speaks throughout the call, and decides when to ask the reasoner for help.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reasoner" icon="fa-light fa-gear">
    Does the task work in the background. It follows your procedures, calls your tools, and returns what it found.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

When the speaker needs something done, it **delegates** to the reasoner. The two run side by side: while the reasoner works, the speaker keeps the conversation going, and when a result arrives, the speaker fits it into the conversation as it stands.

GPT-Live hears and speaks directly, so there's no separate transcriber or text-to-speech provider to configure.

## What changes with GPT-Live

Because the conversation can continue while a tool runs, part of building the assistant is deciding what happens during that time: which work to start early, what to ask while it runs, how to wait well when nothing else can happen, and how to bring the result back in. The same applies to interruptions, corrections, and detours that arrive while work is in progress.

A few things need deliberate design. The speaker only starts work when it delegates, so the triggers need to be explicit. Speech is generated, so exact wording isn't guaranteed. And speaking and acting are separate, so the assistant saying something happened is different from your service confirming it.

As an assistant takes on more kinds of work, [reasoner skills](/gpt-live/skills) let you organize each procedure and its tools behind the same conversation.

## What Vapi provides

Vapi connects GPT-Live to your phone numbers and tools, and provides call records and observability according to your recording and data settings:

- Browser, Twilio, Vapi SIP, and WebSocket calls.
- Function, API request, and MCP tools, plus built-in end-call, transfer, and DTMF tools.
- Speaker and reasoner prompts, reasoner skills, personality packs, and 22 voices.
- Idle-message hooks and HTTP live call control.
- Transcripts, recordings, structured outputs, scorecards, Boards, Monitoring, and Voice Simulations.

Before you commit, check whether your assistant depends on something GPT-Live doesn't support:

| If you need | With GPT-Live |
| --- | --- |
| Exact or prerecorded speech | Not available. All speech is generated |
| A voice from another provider, or a custom voice | Not available. Choose from 22 OpenAI voices |
| A squad with handoffs | Not supported. Use one assistant, often with skills |
| Knowledge bases or the Query tool | Not available. Use a retrieval tool |
| Warm transfers, or transfers on browser calls | Not supported. Blind transfers work on Twilio and Vapi SIP |
| Caller keypad input | Not supported |
| Native Telnyx or Vonage numbers | Not supported |

The full list is in [Settings and compatibility](/gpt-live/configuration#compatibility).

These pages describe Vapi's GPT-Live integration. OpenAI's API and ChatGPT offer some capabilities, such as web search, custom voices, and image input, that aren't available through Vapi. For background on the model itself, see OpenAI's [GPT-Live documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live).

## Access

GPT-Live is in private beta and must be enabled for your organization. If you don't have access yet, [join the waitlist](https://vapi.ai/gpt-live-waitlist). Joining doesn't guarantee early access.

## Cost

A GPT-Live call is billed for voice time by the second, including silence and waiting, plus Vapi's platform fee, the reasoner's token usage, and telephony. See [Cost](/gpt-live/testing#cost) for a worked example and how to check a real call.
