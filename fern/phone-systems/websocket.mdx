Some phone systems send a call's audio to an outside service over a WebSocket, a live two-way connection over the web: Twilio Media Streams, Telnyx media streaming, Vonage's WebSocket connection, Genesys Cloud's Audio Connector, Asterisk's WebSocket channel, and custom dialers. In [Vapi for CX](https://cx.vapi.ai), these systems are **Coming soon**: none of them can be pointed at the agent directly, because each call would need a bridge that someone builds and hosts. [Contact sales](https://vapi.ai/sales) and name the phone system you use.

If your calls come in on a Twilio, Telnyx or Vonage number, you don't need a WebSocket: connect the number instead, and Vapi takes the call itself. See [Twilio](/phone-systems/twilio), [Telnyx](/phone-systems/telnyx) or [Vonage](/phone-systems/vonage). For Genesys Cloud, see [Genesys Cloud](/phone-systems/genesys). If your phone system also speaks SIP (Asterisk, for example), it can send calls to the agent over SIP instead: see [Your own phone system](/phone-systems/own-phone-system).

## What Coming soon covers

- **Calls in:** your phone system would stream each call's audio to the agent through a bridge.
- **Handoffs:** the agent can't transfer a WebSocket call itself, and a WebSocket call has no SIP to transfer over. Your phone system keeps the caller, so it would carry on with its next step when the agent ends its part, without being told why.
- **Calls out:** your dialer would place the call and open the stream. The agent would never dial, so Vapi's caller ID and voicemail detection wouldn't apply.

## Limitations

- The agent can't hear keypad presses on a WebSocket call.
- These calls have no number, so they don't show as a phone line in Vapi for CX.
- There is one phone call, and it stays on your phone system: you pay your phone system for the call and the stream, and Vapi for the agent. Genesys bills for the time its Audio Connector is open.
- These calls count toward your Vapi limit on calls at once.
- Caller audio crosses your bridge, which matters for HIPAA.
