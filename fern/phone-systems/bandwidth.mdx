Send your Bandwidth numbers to Vapi over a SIP trunk (a standing internet phone connection) that you set up in Vapi's dashboard (**Connect in Vapi**), then pick the number in [Vapi for CX](https://cx.vapi.ai).

- **Receive calls:** Through a Voice Configuration Package that routes to the Vapi trunk, as [Bandwidth's Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/) describes.
- **Transfer to a phone number:** Offered. Vapi passes the handoff to Bandwidth.
- **Transfer to a SIP address:** Offered. Vapi passes the handoff to Bandwidth.
- **Return the call to their system:** Only when a contact center or phone system that takes calls back is linked to the line. See [Handing callers to people](/phone-systems#handing-callers-to-people).
- **Place calls:** With a Bandwidth SIP realm and credential. [Bandwidth's Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/) adds them as a second trunk; this page adds them to the trunk you make below instead. The caller ID must be a number on the same Bandwidth account as the realm. See **Place calls from Bandwidth numbers** under [Other options](#other-options).

## Send calls to the agent

You need a Bandwidth Universal Platform account with SIP trunking, a number, and the right to create Voice Configuration Packages.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy Bandwidth's signaling IP address">
    In the Bandwidth App, open **Account › Trunk Group Configurations**. Copy the standard SIP (Orig/Term) address, not the TLS, SIP Auth or Registrar one. If more than one standard SIP address is listed, copy each: the trunk needs a gateway for every one. For more information, see Bandwidth's [Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the SIP trunk in Vapi's dashboard">
    In Vapi's dashboard, open **Keys** and choose **Add New SIP Trunk**. Under **Gateway Configuration**, set **IP Address/Domain** to the signaling IP, **Port** to 5060 and **Netmask** to 32, and turn on **Allow inbound calls**. If you copied more than one signaling IP, add a gateway for each. With one missing, calls work sometimes and fail other times.

    The trunk's **Credential ID** makes the address Bandwidth sends calls to: `<credential id>.sip.vapi.ai`.

    Keep this one trunk for both directions. For calls out, you add a second gateway to it later. Bandwidth's Vapi guide makes a second trunk for calls out; this page doesn't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the Voice Configuration Package">
    In the Bandwidth App, open **Service Management › Voice Configuration** and choose **Create New** (Bandwidth's ElevenLabs guide calls it **Add Package**). Name it, set the route type to **Route to Domain Name**, and enter the trunk's address from the step above. Bandwidth's [Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/) gives this menu path, and its [create a package](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/universal-platform/create-a-vcp/) page makes the same package through the API.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign the numbers to the package">
    In the Bandwidth App, open each number's **Voice settings**, assign it to the package, and confirm that inbound voice is active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add each number in Vapi's dashboard">
    In Vapi's dashboard, open **Phone Numbers**, choose **Create Phone Number**, then **BYO SIP Trunk Number**. Enter each number in full international format with a leading `+`, pick the trunk under **SIP Trunk Credential**, and add a label. For more information, see [Vapi's SIP trunking guide](/advanced/sip/sip-trunk).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the number in Vapi for CX">
    On the agent's setup checklist, open **Connect your phone line**, choose **Use a number already in Vapi**, and pick the number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Number the agent sees:** Bandwidth sends the dialed number with a leading `+`, as [Bandwidth's Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/) describes.

**Check it:** when Vapi for CX shows **Call this number from your phone**, call one of the numbers from your own phone. The check passes when the agent answers. If nothing arrives, the most common cause is the signaling IP: Vapi refuses a call from an address that isn't on the trunk's gateway list, and Bandwidth then reports a 550 error.

**Disconnect.** In Vapi for CX, disconnecting a number set up in Vapi's dashboard only detaches the agent. Nothing changes at Bandwidth or in Vapi's dashboard.

## Hand callers to your team

On a Bandwidth trunk, Vapi passes each handoff to Bandwidth (SIP's own transfer message) rather than placing a new call, for both **Transfer to a phone number** and **Transfer to a SIP address**. Bandwidth decides how it places the call and which number shows to whoever answers. For how handoffs work, see [Handing callers to people](/phone-systems#handing-callers-to-people).

Bandwidth's Vapi guide doesn't cover transfers. Its ElevenLabs guide passes calls to an address written `sip:+1…@bandwidth.com`, where `+1…` is the number to ring, rather than to a plain number. The summary rides as a SIP header, so it doesn't reach a phone number. When Bandwidth rings a phone number this way, the named details may not reach the person who answers either.

## Limitations

- Calls out need SIP credentials, which Bandwidth support or sales may have to turn on. They can't sign in by IP address alone: Vapi's addresses are shared by every Vapi customer.
- Older accounts (Locations, also called SIP Peers) can't connect until they move to the Universal Platform.
- If Bandwidth moves your trunk group, its signaling IP changes, and calls in stop until you edit the gateway in Vapi's dashboard. A refused call leaves no record in Vapi, so Vapi for CX can only say that nothing arrived.
- A Bandwidth number alone doesn't allow **Return the call to their system**. See [Handing callers to people](/phone-systems#handing-callers-to-people).
- Bandwidth bills trunk minutes each way, and Vapi bills its own minutes. One trunk carries calls both ways; more are bought through your Bandwidth account manager. The Build trial reaches only your verified mobile number, in the US and Canada, with five calls at once and 30 minutes a call.
- Vapi doesn't port numbers.

## Other options

<Accordion title="Place calls from Bandwidth numbers">
Calls out go through a realm gateway on the same trunk.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get SIP credentials turned on">
    In the Bandwidth App, open **Account › SIP Credentials**. If the tab is missing, or adding a realm fails, ask Bandwidth support or your account manager to enable SIP credentials on the account. For more information, see Bandwidth's [realms and credentials](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/account/sipRegistrar) page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a realm and a SIP credential">
    Under **SIP Credentials**, choose **Manage Realms**, then **Add**, and pick the trunk group. Then add a SIP credential with a **Username**, **Password** and the **Realm**. Bandwidth generates the realm's host name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the realm to the same trunk in Vapi's dashboard">
    In Vapi's dashboard, open the trunk under **Keys**. Add the realm's host name as a second gateway with **Allow outbound calls** only and **Port** 5060. Under **Authentication (Optional)**, enter the username and password. The username is bare, with no realm added to it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Numbers go out in full international format. [Bandwidth's Vapi guide](https://dev.bandwidth.com/docs/voice/integrations/vapi/) explains its errors: if a call out fails with a 500 error, the request carried no SIP credential, or the account isn't enabled for SIP credentials. A 403, or a repeated 407, means the username, password or realm host name doesn't match.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Other ways to connect Bandwidth">
- **A Programmable Voice (BXML) application that sends the call to SIP.** The only way to add caller details as headers. You build and host the application. Not self-serve.
- **Forward the number to a Vapi number.** A number feature at Bandwidth. It adds a second Bandwidth leg.
- Vapi has no Bandwidth number import or audio streaming.
</Accordion>
