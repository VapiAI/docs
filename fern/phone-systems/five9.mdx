Five9 is a cloud contact center whose IVR scripts (phone menus) can send a caller to an outside number. In [Vapi for CX](https://cx.vapi.ai), Five9 is **Vapi for CX's contact-center screen, then this page**: it connects by phone number. The script's Third Party Transfer module calls the agent's number, and when the agent ends its part, the script can carry on and send the caller to a skill. Five9 connects over SIP with call details only to AI vendors it has accredited, so that route is Coming soon: <a href="https://vapi.ai/sales" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Contact sales</a>.

## At a glance

- **Receiving calls:** Works today. The IVR script's Third Party Transfer module calls the agent's number.
- **Transferring out:** Works today. By phone number today; the return to your script is "Yes, on paper".
- **Calls out:** Doesn't apply. Five9 is your dialer, not a carrier for the agent.
- **Self-serve:** Works today. Your Five9 admin edits and saves one IVR script.

<Note>
Nothing on this page has been proven with a real call yet.
</Note>

## Receiving calls

Five9 keeps the caller's leg and places a second call to the agent's number.

If your organization is in Vapi's EU region, it has no starter number. Connect a phone line first (Twilio, Telnyx or Vonage, or a number set up in Vapi's dashboard) and send Five9's calls to that number.

<Note>Read from Five9's documentation on Nov 22, 2026. Not run.</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the agent's number">
    In Vapi for CX, open the agent, choose **Connect your phone line**, and pick **Five9**. Copy the agent's number in the form your Five9 domain dials: 10 digits with no leading 1, dashes or brackets (for example 4155550124), or, if your domain is enabled for E.164, starting with + and the country code (for example +14155550124).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the campaign's IVR script">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, open the script the campaign uses in the **IVR Scripts** folder. To start a new one, right-click **IVR Scripts** and choose **Add IVR Script**. See Five9's [IVR Administrator's Guide](https://documentation-be.five9.com/bundle/ivr-administrators-guide/raw/resource/enus/ivr-administrators-guide.pdf).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a Third Party Transfer module">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, drag a **Third Party Transfer** module onto the branch the agent should answer. On its **General** tab, under **Third Party Number**, choose **Constant** and paste the agent's number. Five9's guide covers the module on pages 279 to 281.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the two timers">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, on the same tab, set **Max Time of 3rd Party Call (seconds)** (up to 3,600) and **Ringing Timeout (seconds)** (up to 30).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the script">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, choose **Actions**, then **Save**. New calls use the saved script.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Number the agent sees:** unknown. Five9's guide says nothing about caller ID for the Third Party Transfer module. For transfers a person makes, Five9 sends the domain's own number by default, not the caller's (Five9's [Campaign Administrator's Guide](https://documentation-be.five9.com/bundle/campaign-administrators-guide/raw/resource/enus/campaign-administrators-guide.pdf), pages 39 to 40 and 46). If Five9 shows its own number here too, an experiment on the agent would put every call on one version.

<Note>
Unknown. One test call settles it.
</Note>

**Check it:** in Vapi for CX, the check says "Call this number from your phone". Call the campaign's number. The line is marked working when the first call reaches the agent. Then open that call and read the caller's number.

## Handing callers to people

Use "Return the call to their system" to send callers back into your IVR script. Use "Transfer to a phone number" where the reason matters, with one handoff per queue.

**Does a summary travel:** no. The script isn't told why the caller was handed over; sending a summary on the hang-up needs AI Agent Connect.

**Warm handoff (the agent waits with the caller):** doesn't apply. The agent can wait with the caller only on a Twilio number and only for a person's own line; a Five9 number is answered by a script first, and a return makes no transfer.

**Check it:** once the agent's version with the handoff is live, choose **Check** on its row in Vapi for CX. Call the agent from your phone (for a return, the campaign's number) and ask for a person. The check shows "Handoff sent" when the agent hands over or ends its part, then asks whoever answered to confirm. To hear a returned call carry on, put a **Play** module after the Third Party Transfer.

### Transfer to a phone number

Works today. The agent calls a Five9 inbound number (a DNIS) on an inbound campaign whose script sends the caller to a skill. It adds a second Five9 call while the first one is still up.

<Note>Read from Five9's documentation on Nov 22, 2026. Not run.</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a number to an inbound campaign">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, open **Campaign Properties** for an inbound campaign and select the **DNIS** tab. Choose **Add**, pick a number assigned to your account, choose **OK**, then **Save** or **Apply**. A number belongs to one campaign, and numbers already on another inbound campaign don't appear. See Five9's [Campaign Administrator's Guide](https://documentation-be.five9.com/bundle/campaign-administrators-guide/raw/resource/enus/campaign-administrators-guide.pdf), pages 58 to 61.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the caller to a skill">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, give that campaign a script that goes straight to a **Skill Transfer** module with no prompts. It must not lead back to the agent's number, or the call loops.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the handoff on the agent">
    In Vapi for CX, on the agent's **Handing callers to people** card, choose **Add a handoff**. Say when the agent should hand over, choose **Transfer to a phone number**, and paste the Five9 number. Under **More options**, choose that their people see the caller's number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Number their people see:** the caller's own number on a Twilio line, or when the handoff is set to show it. Otherwise the agent's number, and on a starter, SIP-address or trunk line the carrier may still refuse a caller's number the line doesn't own.

### Return the call to their system

Yes, on paper, with Five9's **Return After 3rd Party Call** setting on. The agent ends its part of the call, and Five9, which never let go of the caller, carries on to the next module in your script. Nothing tells Five9 why or which queue, so every returned caller follows the script's default path and may be asked again. Where the reason matters, add a separate "Transfer to a phone number" per queue.

<Note>
Needs a test call: whether the script carries on cleanly when the agent ends a call that reached it over the public phone network.
</Note>

<Note>Read from Five9's documentation on Nov 22, 2026. Not run.</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on Return After 3rd Party Call">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, open the IVR script that sends callers to the agent, then its **Third Party Transfer** module, **General** tab. Turn on **Return After 3rd Party Call**. Five9's words: "Whether to return the call to your domain after the third-party interaction is completed."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect a next module">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, connect a next module after the Third Party Transfer, for example a **Skill Transfer** to the queue every returned caller should reach. Without a next module, Five9 reports error 2705, "Third party transfer does not have a descendant".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the script">
    In Five9's VCC Administrator, choose **Actions**, then **Save**. New calls use the saved script.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the handoff on the agent">
    In Vapi for CX, on the agent's **Handing callers to people** card, choose **Add a handoff**. Say when the agent should hand back and choose **Return the call to their system**. There is nowhere to send it: the agent ends its part of the call. The caller also returns by pressing the **Digit to Interrupt 3rd Party Call and Return** key, if one is set, or when **Max Time of 3rd Party Call** runs out.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Number their people see:** the original caller, the original campaign and every Five9 call variable. The call never left Five9.

## Calls out

Doesn't apply. Five9 hands calls to the agent; it isn't the agent's carrier. Calls the agent places go out on a phone line connected in Vapi for CX.

## What can't be done, and what is not yet known

Can't be done:

- AI Agent Connect: Five9 has not accredited Vapi. Five9's guide: "You can implement only pre-integrated, accredited AI providers."
- A SIP transfer message (REFER) to Five9.
- An agent call longer than 3,600 seconds, or ringing longer than 30 seconds.

Not yet known:

- Which number Five9 shows the agent as the caller on a Third Party Transfer call. One test call settles it, and whether an experiment would put every call on one version.
- Whether the script carries on cleanly when the agent ends the call. The same call, with a **Play** module after the transfer, settles it.

## Costs and limits

- Five9 holds the caller's call and pays for the call to the agent for the whole conversation, with Vapi minutes on top. Long-distance rates apply to the call to the agent.
- A handoff by number, instead of a return, adds a second Five9 inbound call while the first is still up.
- Each conversation with the agent is capped at 60 minutes.
- AI Agent Connect is a paid Five9 add-on on top of the vendor's own price.
- Recording: Five9 says you are responsible for telling the other party that a call is recorded and for getting consent where the law requires it.
- HIPAA: nothing Five9-specific was found.
